What the brain actually controls

The head now has an automatic food-tracking reflex and a visible gaze ray (green when aligned). Toggle auto gaze off to return head control to the network. The 32-input brain is unchanged. One shared neural network reads 32 signals including food direction, body heading, joint angles, body tilt, angular velocities, vertical speed, foot heights and contact. It outputs three-axis targets for every available joint, including the torso, neck, arms and legs. A finite-friction foot-contact model now resists foot slip and applies bounded ground impulses; rapid spinning feet have less grip. A gravity-driven body now has pitch, roll, vertical velocity, approximate center of mass, and foot-ground contacts. Unsupported or unstable poses fall and end the episode. This remains an articulated approximation, not a full constrained rigid-body ragdoll. The torso uses a collision proxy.

Free-movement mode: no balance curriculum, no upright bonus, and no penalty or episode termination for falling. Food approach and collection determine fitness. Ground contact is now impulse-based with finite friction rather than proximity-based traction and foot-height teleportation. This is an intermediate physics upgrade, not yet a full rigid-body ragdoll. Each trial runs a few seconds. Five networks compete on the same five fixed targets per generation; the champion must earn its place again. Watch whether food approaches become more reliable across episodes. Movement can still be random or exploit the simplified physics.
