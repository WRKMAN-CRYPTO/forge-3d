What the brain actually controls

One shared tiny neural network reads food direction, body heading, joint angles and foot contact. It outputs hip, knee, ankle and arm targets. A simple balance/ground-contact approximation translates alternating foot pushes into motion. This is not full rigid-body physics.

Each trial runs a few seconds. The best-performing network survives; new trials mutate its weights. Watch whether food approaches become more reliable across episodes. Movement can still be random or exploit the simplified physics.
